Questions people actually ask

Is “unlimited” really unlimited?

Unlimited describes the queue, not my hours. You can add as many requests as you like; I work through them one (or two) at a time, in the order you set. Most clients see six to twelve substantial pieces of work a month.

What counts as one request?

A screen set, a flow, a landing page, a deck, a component set, a research round. If something is really three jobs wearing a trench coat, I will say so and we will split it.

Who owns the work?

You do, from the moment it is delivered — Figma files, source assets and any code I write. No licensing, no hostage files if you leave.

What do you not do?

Illustration at scale, motion-heavy video editing, and print production. I will happily recommend people who are better at those than I am.

Can I pause?

Any month, with a week of notice. Unused days stay on your account for up to a year, so a quiet quarter does not cost you anything.

How do we start?

A 30-minute call to check the fit, then a shared board and a first request the same week. If the first two weeks do not work for you, I refund them.

About — fifteen years in

I design the parts nobody demos

Vanakkam. I'm Nethra Balasubramaniam, a product designer working out of Chennai with teams across three time zones.

Fifteen years, roughly two hundred projects, ten-odd domains. What that adds up to is a fairly narrow specialism: I'm good at the unglamorous middle of a product — the empty state, the resumable form, the permission model, the review loop — which is where products are actually won or abandoned.

I started on the agency side drawing brand work, moved into enterprise UX at Accenture and Tech Mahindra where I learned to design inside constraints I couldn't negotiate, then spent five years leading product design in studio and client teams. Since 2021 I've worked independently, mostly with GenAI, FinTech and HealthTech teams who need someone senior in the room early.

I also write front-end. Not because designers must code, but because implementing my own interfaces taught me which details survive hand-off and which quietly don't.
